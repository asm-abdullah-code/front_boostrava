Banner Advertising Cost in Bangladesh: Formats, Pricing and Value

A Bangladesh-focused guide to banner advertising cost, covering CPM pricing, banner sizes, premium placements, mobile formats, campaign budgeting, cost drivers, and how advertisers can measure real value.

S
Shaidur Rahman
Programmatic Ads Eng • September 07, 2026

💡 Quick Answer (Summary)

Banner advertising cost in Bangladesh varies by banner format, publisher quality, placement, audience targeting, geography, campaign volume, and buying model. Standard banner inventory is commonly priced on a CPM basis, while premium homepage or sponsored placements may use fixed-time or negotiated pricing. Advertisers should evaluate total campaign cost and business outcomes—not CPM alone—to determine whether a banner campaign offers good value.

  • ✓Banner advertising in Bangladesh does not have one universal rate; pricing changes according to format, placement, inventory quality, targeting, volume, and campaign timing.
  • ✓Common display formats include 300×250, 320×50, 320×100, 728×90, 300×600, 970×90, and 970×250, although availability varies by publisher and device.
  • ✓CPM is useful for comparing media cost, but it does not measure audience relevance, viewability, lead quality, sales, or overall campaign value.

  • ✓Creative production, multiple banner sizes, animation, landing pages, tracking, and campaign management should be included when calculating the real all-in cost.
  • ✓Bangladesh advertisers should give particular attention to mobile banner usability, regional targeting, frequency control, and seasonal demand around Ramadan, Eid, Pahela Baishakh, and major commercial periods.

Banner advertising remains one of the most recognizable forms of digital advertising in Bangladesh.

It is also one of the easiest formats to misunderstand.

A business may hear that banner advertising costs BDT 50 CPM, BDT 150 CPM, BDT 300 CPM, or even several thousand taka for a fixed placement and assume one of those figures must be the “real” market price.

In practice, banner advertising cost in Bangladesh and programmatic ads varies because advertisers are not always buying the same thing.

A standard mobile banner on broad inventory is very different from a premium homepage placement.

A run-of-network campaign is different from targeting finance readers in Dhaka.

A basic image banner is different from an animated HTML5 unit.

A campaign purchased through programmatic bidding is different from a direct fixed-duration booking.

That means the right question is not simply:

“How much does banner advertising cost in Bangladesh?”

A more useful question is:

“What format, inventory, audience, placement, buying model, and campaign outcome am I paying for?”

For Bangladesh advertisers, that distinction matters because digital media budgets are increasingly expected to deliver measurable business value—not only impressions.

This guide breaks down the main banner formats, pricing structures, budget calculations, cost drivers, and value considerations brands should understand before buying display inventory in Bangladesh.

Banner Advertising Cost in Bangladesh: The Practical Answer

There is no single nationwide banner advertising rate.

Pricing can vary significantly according to:

  • Publisher quality
  • Ad position
  • Banner size
  • Mobile or desktop inventory
  • Campaign volume
  • Geographic targeting
  • Audience targeting
  • Content category
  • Seasonality
  • Buying method
  • Creative type
  • Negotiated commercial terms

Public Bangladesh publisher rate cards show that banner inventory can be offered through several different pricing models, including CPM, fixed daily or monthly fees, and premium placement packages.

Because those rate cards represent specific publishers rather than the entire market, they should be treated as reference points—not universal prices.

For planning purposes, brands should separate banner advertising cost into three broad categories:

Standard Display Inventory

Usually the most scalable and flexible option.

Often purchased on a CPM basis.

Premium Display Inventory

Higher-value positions, stronger publisher environments, or more prominent placements.

Usually costs more than standard inventory.

Fixed or Sponsored Placements

Homepage, section sponsorship, masthead, or reserved placements.

These may be sold by:

  • Hour
  • Day
  • Week
  • Month
  • Fixed campaign package

The pricing model matters almost as much as the headline price.

What Is Banner Advertising?

Banner advertising refers to graphical advertisements displayed within websites, digital content environments, and other compatible online inventory.

A banner may contain:

  • Brand logo
  • Product image
  • Headline
  • Offer
  • Call to action
  • Animation
  • HTML5 elements

Banner advertising can support objectives such as:

  • Brand awareness
  • Product discovery
  • Website traffic
  • Lead generation
  • Retargeting
  • E-commerce sales
  • Seasonal campaigns
  • Product launches

The format itself is relatively simple.

The strategic value comes from where the banner appears, who sees it, what it communicates, and what happens after the impression or click.

The Main Banner Advertising Formats Used in Bangladesh

Different banner sizes serve different placements and devices.

The following are common planning formats rather than a guarantee that every publisher supports every size.

Banner Format Typical Size Common Use
Medium Rectangle 300×250 In-content, sidebar, mobile-compatible inventory
Large Rectangle 336×280 Article and desktop content environments
Leaderboard 728×90 Desktop header or content-area placements
Mobile Banner 320×50 Mobile web inventory
Large Mobile Banner 320×100 Higher-impact mobile placement
Half Page 300×600 Premium desktop/sidebar visibility
Billboard 970×250 High-impact desktop placement
Wide Leaderboard 970×90 Desktop header inventory

IAB Tech Lab's display advertising standards have long helped the industry organize digital ad units around common sizes and flexible creative principles.

For advertisers, standardization matters because it can reduce production cost and make creative easier to deploy across multiple publishers and programmatic inventory sources.

Why Banner Size Affects Cost

The cost difference between banner sizes is not simply based on pixels.

Larger or more prominent units may cost more because they can offer:

  • Greater visibility
  • More page space
  • Lower supply availability
  • Higher advertiser demand
  • Better creative storytelling
  • Stronger attention potential

A 300×250 unit can exist across a large number of placements.

A large homepage billboard may be available only once or a few times on a page.

Scarcity changes commercial value.

That is why advertisers should not assume that all banner impressions should have the same CPM.

How Banner Advertising Is Priced in Bangladesh

There are several common ways digital publishers and advertising platforms can price banner campaigns.

CPM Pricing

CPM means cost per 1,000 impressions.

It is one of the most common digital advertising pricing methods.

The formula is:

Banner Media Cost = Impressions ÷ 1,000 × CPM

Suppose a campaign buys:

  • 1,000,000 impressions
  • BDT 100 CPM

The media spend is:

1,000,000 ÷ 1,000 × 100 = BDT 100,000

CPM is useful because it makes campaigns with different impression volumes easier to compare.

But CPM alone does not measure business value.

CPC Pricing

Some campaigns may be evaluated or purchased around cost per click.

CPC formula:

CPC = Total Spend ÷ Number of Clicks

If an advertiser spends BDT 20,000 and receives 1,000 clicks:

CPC = BDT 20

CPC helps measure traffic efficiency.

However, a cheap click does not necessarily mean a valuable visitor.

A higher CPC campaign can still produce better returns if the visitors are more likely to buy.

Fixed-Time Pricing

Premium banner inventory may be sold for a fixed period.

Examples can include:

  • Homepage banner for 24 hours
  • Section takeover for one week
  • Header banner for one month
  • Event-day placement

Fixed pricing can make sense when the advertiser values guaranteed visibility within a specific publisher environment.

Unlike CPM, the number of impressions may vary depending on publisher traffic during the booked period.

Fixed Placement Packages

Publishers may also bundle several placements together.

For example:

  • Homepage leaderboard
  • Article rectangle
  • Mobile banner
  • Section sponsorship

A package may look expensive compared with one standard CPM placement but could include inventory that is not directly comparable.

Advertisers should request a breakdown when possible.

What Actually Determines Banner CPM?

The quoted CPM is influenced by more than banner dimensions.

Several factors can materially change price.

1. Publisher Quality

Inventory from a trusted, established, professionally managed publisher may command a higher rate than low-quality or unknown inventory.

Brands may value:

  • Editorial quality
  • Audience loyalty
  • Brand suitability
  • Better viewability
  • Transparent inventory

The quality of the environment surrounding the advertisement can affect the value of the impression.

2. Placement Position

A banner above the fold may have different economics from an ad far down the page.

Potential placement differences include:

  • Top of page
  • In-article
  • Sidebar
  • Sticky position
  • Footer
  • Homepage
  • Category page

Higher visibility does not guarantee better performance, but premium positions are often priced accordingly.

3. Audience Targeting

Targeting can increase the value and sometimes the cost of inventory.

Examples include:

  • Business audiences
  • Technology readers
  • Property audiences
  • Geographic segments
  • Returning site visitors
  • High-intent users

A narrow audience may be more commercially valuable but less available.

That can create a pricing premium.

4. Geographic Targeting

A campaign targeting all of Bangladesh may have more available inventory than a campaign targeting only selected users in Dhaka, Chattogram, or Sylhet.

Geographic restrictions can change:

  • Reach
  • Availability
  • CPM
  • Campaign pacing

Advertisers should only add geographic restrictions when they are commercially justified.

5. Campaign Volume

Larger campaigns may receive different commercial terms.

For example, a brand buying several million impressions may negotiate differently from a small advertiser purchasing a limited test campaign.

Volume can influence:

  • CPM
  • Placement access
  • Reporting
  • Added value
  • Creative support

6. Campaign Timing

Demand can increase around important commercial periods in Bangladesh.

Examples include:

  • Ramadan
  • Eid-ul-Fitr
  • Eid-ul-Adha
  • Pahela Baishakh
  • Wedding season
  • Major sports events
  • Product launch periods
  • Year-end sales periods

Premium banner availability may become more limited during peak demand.

Early planning can therefore affect both price and inventory choice.

Standard Banner vs Premium Banner Pricing

It is useful to distinguish between low-cost scalable inventory and premium inventory.

Standard Banner Advertising

Often best for:

  • Reach
  • Frequency
  • Prospecting
  • Performance testing
  • Retargeting
  • Always-on campaigns

The value comes from scalable delivery.

Premium Banner Advertising

Often best for:

  • Product launches
  • Major seasonal campaigns
  • Brand awareness
  • High-visibility campaigns
  • Sponsorship-like activity

The value comes from prominence and environment.

Neither is automatically better.

The right choice depends on campaign objective.

Banner Advertising Cost vs Rich Media Cost

Banner advertising and rich media advertising are related but not identical.

A standard banner may use:

  • Static image
  • Simple animation
  • Basic HTML5 and programmatic ads

Rich media may involve:

  • Expansion
  • Interaction
  • Video
  • Scroll response
  • Multiple creative states
  • Custom coding

That generally makes rich media more expensive to produce and sometimes more expensive to buy.

For brands comparing the two:

Cost Area Standard Banner Rich Media
Creative Production Lower Higher
Technical QA Lower Higher
Media Availability Broad More limited
Interaction Basic Advanced
File Complexity Lower Higher
Campaign Setup Simpler More complex

A brand should not pay for rich media complexity if a standard banner communicates the message effectively.

What Does a Banner Campaign Really Cost?

A common mistake is to look only at media cost.

The complete campaign budget can include:

  • Media spend
  • Banner design
  • Multiple sizes
  • Animation
  • Landing-page design
  • Tracking
  • Campaign management
  • Creative adaptation

A campaign with BDT 100,000 in media may actually cost BDT 120,000–150,000 or more depending on production requirements.

That is why brands should calculate all-in campaign cost.

A Practical Banner Budget Example

Imagine a Bangladesh retailer planning a campaign.

Campaign plan:

  • 2,000,000 impressions
  • BDT 90 CPM
  • Four banner sizes
  • BDT 30,000 creative production cost

Media cost:

2,000,000 ÷ 1,000 × BDT 90 = BDT 180,000

Creative cost:

BDT 30,000

Total campaign cost:

BDT 210,000

All-in effective CPM:

BDT 105

The advertiser may have purchased media at BDT 90 CPM, but the total economic cost of the campaign is approximately BDT 105 per 1,000 impressions.

That figure can be more useful when comparing campaigns.

Why Creative Cost Matters More for Small Campaigns

Suppose creative production costs BDT 30,000.

If a campaign delivers 300,000 impressions, creative cost alone adds:

BDT 100 per 1,000 impressions

If the same creative is used across 3 million impressions, the production cost contributes:

BDT 10 per 1,000 impressions

Campaign scale changes the economics.

Small advertisers can often control cost by:

  • Using standard sizes
  • Keeping animation simple
  • Reusing a master design system
  • Avoiding unnecessary creative versions

Static vs Animated Banner Cost

Static banners are usually simpler to produce.

They may require:

  • Design
  • Copy
  • Product image
  • CTA

Animated banners require more work.

They may include:

  • Multiple frames
  • Motion timing
  • Transition design
  • HTML5 or GIF production
  • Additional QA

Animation should only be added if it improves communication.

Too much movement can reduce clarity.

Mobile Banner Advertising Cost in Bangladesh

Mobile is especially important in Bangladesh because the digital audience is heavily mobile-oriented.

Mobile banner campaigns may use formats such as:

  • 320×50
  • 320×100
  • 300×250

The actual CPM depends on inventory and targeting rather than mobile format alone.

Advertisers should evaluate:

  • Mobile viewability
  • Landing-page speed
  • Thumb-friendly CTA
  • Screen readability
  • Conversion flow

A low-cost mobile impression has limited value if the destination page performs poorly on mobile.

Homepage Banner Advertising Cost

Homepage inventory is often more expensive because it offers:

  • High visibility
  • High traffic concentration
  • Limited supply
  • Strong branding value

Homepage pricing may be structured as:

  • Fixed daily fee
  • Hourly placement
  • CPM
  • Campaign package

For a brand launch, a homepage banner may make sense.

For continuous customer acquisition, broader programmatic display may be more efficient.

The buying decision should match the campaign objective.

Sidebar Banner Cost

Sidebar inventory is common on desktop.

It may support formats such as:

  • 300×250
  • 300×600

Its value depends on:

  • Page layout
  • Reader behavior
  • Scroll position
  • Viewability

Advertisers should not assume a large sidebar automatically provides strong attention.

Viewability data matters.

In-Article Banner Cost

In-article banners appear within editorial content.

These can be valuable because they are integrated into the reading flow.

Potential advantages include:

  • Stronger visibility
  • Natural placement
  • Better mobile adaptability

But aggressive in-content ad density can damage the reading experience.

Quality publishers should balance revenue with usability.

What Makes a Banner Impression Valuable?

An impression has value when it has a reasonable opportunity to influence the intended audience.

Useful quality indicators can include:

  • Viewability
  • Audience relevance
  • Content relevance
  • Device compatibility
  • Brand safety
  • Geographic relevance
  • Frequency
  • Human traffic quality

This is why the cheapest CPM may not produce the best campaign.

Cheap CPM vs Valuable CPM

Consider two hypothetical options.

Metric Campaign A Campaign B
CPM BDT 50 BDT 120
Impressions 2,000,000 1,000,000
Spend BDT 100,000 BDT 120,000
Qualified Visits 2,000 5,000
Sales 20 75

Campaign A delivers twice as many impressions.

Campaign B produces far more sales.

The “expensive” CPM created better business value.

This is why CPM should be treated as a media cost metric—not a final performance metric.

Cost Per Qualified Visit Is Often More Useful Than CPC

A campaign can generate accidental or low-quality clicks.

For that reason, advertisers may want to compare:

Cost per qualified visit = Ad spend ÷ Qualified visits

A qualified visit might require:

  • Minimum session time
  • Product-page view
  • Multiple page views
  • Meaningful on-site action

This creates a better bridge between media and business performance.

Frequency Can Affect Banner Advertising Value

Showing an ad repeatedly to the same person can eventually reduce incremental value.

A campaign should monitor:

  • Reach
  • Frequency
  • CTR by frequency
  • Conversion by frequency
  • Creative fatigue

A user seeing the same banner 25 times is not necessarily 25 times more likely to buy.

Frequency controls can help reduce wasted impressions.

Retargeting Banner Cost

Retargeting can be valuable because it focuses on people who have already interacted with the advertiser.

Potential audiences include:

  • Website visitors
  • Product viewers
  • Cart abandoners
  • Lead-form visitors
  • Previous customers

Retargeting audiences are often smaller and more commercially valuable than broad prospecting audiences.

The advertiser should evaluate cost against conversion probability rather than simply comparing CPM.

Banner Advertising for E-commerce Brands

E-commerce brands can use banners for:

  • Product discovery
  • Seasonal promotions
  • Cart recovery
  • New arrivals
  • Category promotion
  • Retargeting

The most important measurement should go beyond clicks.

Useful metrics include:

  • Add-to-cart rate
  • Checkout rate
  • Delivered orders
  • Revenue
  • Cost per order
  • Contribution margin

In Bangladesh, cash-on-delivery businesses should be especially careful not to treat every submitted order as equal final revenue.

Banner Advertising for Local Businesses

Local businesses can use display advertising for:

  • Local awareness
  • Store traffic
  • Lead generation
  • Service inquiries
  • Event promotion

Geographic targeting may make smaller budgets more useful.

A business that only serves Dhaka does not need to pay for irrelevant impressions across the country.

Banner Advertising for Brand Campaigns

Brand campaigns may care about:

  • Reach
  • Frequency
  • Premium environments
  • Viewability
  • Visibility during key periods

A higher CPM may be acceptable if the campaign needs prominent exposure.

For example, a brand may prioritize premium banner inventory around:

  • Ramadan
  • Eid
  • Pahela Baishakh
  • Major product launches

The value comes from timing and impact rather than direct-response efficiency alone.

How to Compare Banner Advertising Quotes

Advertisers should not compare two quotes only by total price.

Ask whether both proposals include the same:

  • Impression volume
  • Banner sizes
  • Placement
  • Publisher quality
  • Geography
  • Audience
  • Frequency
  • Campaign period
  • Creative production
  • Reporting
  • Tracking

A BDT 50 CPM proposal and a BDT 150 CPM proposal may be buying completely different media.

Questions to Ask Before Buying Banner Advertising

Before approving a campaign, ask:

  1. Is pricing CPM-based or fixed?
  2. What banner sizes are included?
  3. Is mobile inventory included?
  4. Which placements will be used?
  5. What geographic targeting is available?
  6. Can frequency be controlled?
  7. Is creative production included?
  8. What reporting will be provided?
  9. Is conversion tracking supported?
  10. Can poor-performing placements be optimized?
  11. Is inventory transparent?
  12. What happens if the campaign under-delivers?

These questions make the commercial proposal easier to evaluate.

How Brands Can Reduce Banner Advertising Cost Without Reducing Quality

Cost control does not always require choosing cheaper inventory.

Brands can improve efficiency in several ways.

Reuse Standard Creative Sizes

Common formats reduce adaptation cost.

Focus on Relevant Markets

Do not buy geography that the business cannot serve.

Improve Landing Pages

Better conversion rates allow the same media budget to produce more customers.

Control Frequency

Avoid excessive repeat impressions.

Test Multiple Creatives

Weak creative can make good inventory look bad.

Move Budget Toward Productive Inventory

Optimization can reduce waste while preserving quality.

The objective should be better value, not simply lower CPM.

When Is Banner Advertising Worth the Money?

Banner advertising is most useful when it has a clear job.

It can be valuable for:

  • Creating reach
  • Supporting product launches
  • Generating qualified traffic
  • Retargeting interested users
  • Building brand familiarity
  • Supporting seasonal campaigns
  • Reaching relevant content audiences

It is less useful when:

  • The offer is unclear
  • The landing page is poor
  • Tracking is missing
  • The campaign targets irrelevant users
  • The creative is unreadable
  • The advertiser evaluates only clicks

The format itself does not determine success.

Execution does.

Banner Advertising Value Should Be Measured Beyond CPM

The most important lesson in banner advertising pricing is that cost and value are not the same thing.

CPM answers:

How much did the impressions cost?

It does not answer:

  • Were the impressions viewable?
  • Did they reach the right audience?
  • Did they create qualified visits?
  • Did they generate leads?
  • Did they produce sales?
  • Did they support brand objectives?

A useful campaign dashboard can therefore include:

Campaign Objective Useful Metrics
Awareness Reach, frequency, viewability
Traffic Qualified visits, CPC
Leads Qualified leads, CPL
E-commerce Orders, CAC, revenue
Retargeting Conversion rate, CPA
Brand campaign Reach quality, attention, engagement

Cost should always be interpreted in relation to the objective.

Final Perspective: What Should Brands Actually Pay For?

Banner advertising in Bangladesh can be inexpensive or premium.

Both can be good buys.

Both can also waste money.

The difference comes from what the advertiser receives in return.

Brands should pay for:

  • Relevant audiences
  • Suitable placements
  • Quality publisher environments
  • Strong viewability
  • Appropriate frequency
  • Effective creative
  • Useful measurement

They should not pay simply for a large number of cheap impressions.

A BDT 60 CPM campaign may be excellent if it reaches the right people and drives business value.

A BDT 250 CPM campaign may also be excellent if premium ads placement delivers the visibility the brand needs.

And either campaign can fail if the audience, creative, landing page, or measurement is poor.

For Bangladesh advertisers, the best way to evaluate banner advertising cost is therefore not to search for one “correct” market price.

Instead, compare:

format + placement + audience + volume + campaign objective + total cost + measurable outcome

That is the combination that determines whether banner advertising is genuinely expensive—or genuinely valuable.

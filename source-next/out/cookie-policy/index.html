Cookie Policy

How Boost RAVA uses cookies, local storage, pixels, tags and similar technologies across its website and advertising technology services.

Effective: 30 August 2026 · Last updated: 30 August 2026

Boost RAVA LegalPrivacy PolicyTerms & ConditionsPayment PolicyCookie PolicyContact
Cookie and advertising technologies can vary by page, account, publisher integration, campaign configuration, browser and jurisdiction. This policy describes categories and purposes; it does not claim that every technology is deployed in every context.
01

1. What cookies and similar technologies are

Cookies are small data files stored by a browser. Similar technologies include local storage, pixels, tags, scripts, SDK-like components and identifiers used to operate websites, remember settings, measure events, secure services or support advertising workflows.

Some technologies are first-party, meaning they are set in connection with Boost RAVA services. Others may be provided by a publisher, advertiser, infrastructure or service provider involved in a requested workflow.

02

2. Why Boost RAVA may use them

  • Keep sessions and account functions working
  • Protect login, security and fraud-prevention workflows
  • Remember language, interface or consent preferences
  • Measure website and platform performance
  • Support campaign delivery, frequency or event measurement where configured
  • Associate eligible ad requests, impressions, clicks or conversion events where permitted
  • Diagnose errors, latency and technical problems
  • Understand aggregate usage and improve service design
03

3. Cookie categories

Strictly necessary

Used for essential operation, security, authentication, session continuity, load management or other functions necessary to provide a requested service. Disabling these through browser settings may cause parts of the service to stop working.

Preferences and functionality

Used to remember choices or interface settings so a returning user receives a more consistent experience.

Analytics and performance

Used to understand aggregate usage, page or feature performance, errors and service quality. Where required, these technologies are subject to applicable consent choices.

Advertising and measurement

Where implemented and legally permitted, advertising technologies may support ad delivery, frequency, campaign measurement, conversion or postback workflows, contextual or eligible targeting signals and reporting. Their use depends on the campaign, publisher environment, browser and applicable consent or legal requirements.

04

4. Programmatic advertising context

An ad request can contain technical information needed to evaluate an eligible campaign, such as placement, page context, device or browser characteristics, approximate geography, time, campaign rules and identifiers. Programmatic systems can use those signals to select or measure an ad in a short period of time.

Boost RAVA does not state that every programmatic request uses a cookie or personal profile. Contextual advertising can use page or content context, and some delivery or measurement can operate with limited identifiers depending on configuration and law.

05

5. Pixels, tags and event measurement

Advertisers or publishers may configure tags, pixels, JavaScript, server-to-server postbacks or other event mechanisms where supported. These can record events such as an ad request, impression, click, page action or conversion. The party deploying a tag is responsible for having the necessary authority, notices and consent where required.

06

6. Consent and legal basis

Where applicable law requires consent before non-essential cookies or similar technologies are used, the responsible party should obtain and respect that consent. Where another lawful basis applies, processing should still be transparent and proportionate. Publisher and advertiser implementations must not use Boost RAVA technology to bypass consent requirements.

07

7. Your choices

  • Use available cookie or consent controls on the relevant website
  • Change browser settings to block, delete or limit cookies
  • Use device or browser privacy controls where available
  • Avoid optional advertising or analytics technologies when a valid opt-out is offered
  • Contact Boost RAVA about technologies used directly on Boost RAVA-controlled pages
08

8. What happens if cookies are blocked

Blocking cookies can reduce tracking and persistence, but it may also prevent login, preferences, security checks, measurement or other functionality from operating as intended. Blocking a cookie does not necessarily stop all server-side logs, contextual processing or essential technical requests.

09

9. Third-party technologies

Publisher sites, advertiser landing pages and integrated services may use their own cookies or similar technologies. Those parties control their independent practices and should provide their own notices. Boost RAVA is not responsible for a third party’s independent use merely because an advertising or technical relationship exists.

10

10. Retention and security

Cookie lifetimes and related log retention depend on purpose, configuration and legal requirements. Session technologies may expire quickly, while preference, security or measurement records can be retained longer when reasonably necessary. We apply safeguards appropriate to the relevant systems, but no browser or internet technology is completely risk-free.

11

11. Do Not Track and browser signals

Browsers and jurisdictions can provide different privacy signals. Where a legally binding signal applies to a particular processing activity, it should be handled as required by applicable law. A general browser “Do Not Track” setting does not have a single universally accepted technical meaning across all advertising systems.

12

12. Updates and contact

We may update this Cookie Policy when technologies, platform functionality or legal requirements change. The date at the top identifies the current published version.

Questions about these policies can be submitted through the Boost RAVA Contact page. Please include enough account, campaign, publisher-property, billing, or technical context for us to identify the matter without sending passwords or unnecessary sensitive information.
